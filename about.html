About TrustURL

A small tool for looking at a link before you open it.

What it does

You paste a link, and TrustURL reports what it can learn from the address itself: whether it uses encryption, how the site name is built, whether it hides its destination behind an @ sign or a numeric address, and whether a reputation service lists it as dangerous.

Each finding says whether it is a direct observation of the link or a result from an external service, so you can judge how much weight it deserves.

Limitations

  • TrustURL never visits the link. It cannot see page content, redirects, certificates, or how old the domain is.
  • Warning signs are not proof. Many harmless links look unusual, and many harmful links look ordinary.
  • "No known threats detected" means the address is not on a known-threat list right now. New attacks are often not listed yet, so it is never a guarantee of safety.
  • When no reputation service is configured, or it cannot be reached, the report says so and is marked incomplete.
  • Short links from link-shortening services are not expanded, so the real destination stays hidden.
  • TrustURL does not replace antivirus software, browser protection, or professional threat intelligence.
